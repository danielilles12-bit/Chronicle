Midjourney finds the world. You choose it. Recraft manufactures it. Claude ships it.

  2. Steal like a designer — real references first (1 evening)
  3. Midjourney — shoot the same 5 shots in all three worlds (1 weekend afternoon)
  4. Hand over the keepers — Claude distills briefs & rebuilds 3 home screens (overnight)
  5. The decision gate — pick one world (30 min)
  6. Recraft — production asset kit for the winner (1 evening)
  7. Build — Claude implements from the brief (overnight loops)
The fast lane (~30 min of your time): skip Phase 2, open assets/brand-v2/speedrun-prompts.txt, paste its 12 prompts into Midjourney in one sitting with Draft Mode on (~10 min), come back later and bulk-download everything that isn't terrible into assets/brand-v2/inbox (~10 min), then tell Claude. Claude sorts them by direction and builds a phone-friendly keep/kill page; you swipe (~10 min). Style-reference convergence and the real-artifact staging shots happen later, only for the finalists.

Why this round will be different

The last round failed for a diagnosable reason: we asked writing models to paint. Claude and ChatGPT can reason about design but they generate visuals from words, which converges on "competent generic" — and you judged the mockups as empty rooms, because they were. Your references (Sotheby's, Soane's Museum) get their power from real material photographed beautifully.

A designer's process separates three jobs that we previously mashed together:

  • Finding the world — high-variance visual exploration. Midjourney's job. It produces hundreds of options cheaply; most are bad; that's the point.
  • Choosing — ruthless curation. Your job, and only yours. This is the artistic judgment that was missing. You are not "giving feedback on an output" anymore; you are the art director killing 90% of the film.
  • Manufacturing — turning the chosen world into consistent, production-grade assets (vectors, seals, ornaments, textures). Recraft's job. This is what replaces the hand-drawn SVG art that hit its quality ceiling four versions in a row.
One rule carries over from last round: the soul of the app is your 389 real artifacts. Midjourney builds the stage, never the collection. The Portland Vase stays real.

Phase 2 · Steal like a designer

One evening · 60–90 minutes · no AI involved

Every good designer starts with theft, not prompts. Before generating anything, collect 10–15 real photographs per direction from Google Images or Pinterest. These calibrate your eye and become Midjourney style-reference fuel later.

The only rule: save an image only if it makes you feel something. "It's relevant" is not a reason. "I want to be in that room" is.

Phase 3 · Midjourney — shoot three worlds

One weekend afternoon · 2–3 hours · the fun part

How to work the machine

  • Type prompts in the bar at midjourney.com; results land in Create. Click any image → Vary (more like this) or Upscale (finished quality).
  • Turn on Draft Mode while exploring — images are faster and cheaper. When you find a keeper, hit Enhance to re-render it at full quality.
  • To push new images toward ones you love: drag an image into the prompt bar and mark it as a Style Reference. This is the single most important feature for us — it's how a direction becomes coherent instead of a grab-bag.
  • Drag an image in as an Image/Omni Reference instead when you want the object itself to appear (this is how you put the actual Portland Vase on stage).

Prompt craft — five rules

  1. Describe a photograph, not a design. Name the camera feeling, the light, the materials: "photograph, one green-shaded lamp, dark walnut, dust in the light."
  2. Nouns and light, never adjectives. Banned words: beautiful, epic, intricate, stunning, hyperdetailed, 8k, masterpiece. These produce the AI-slop look you hate.
  3. One idea per image. If a prompt has two ideas, split it into two prompts.
  4. Never ask it for UI screens, logos, or text. It invents fake buttons and misspells everything. Screens get built in code; logos come later in Recraft.
  5. End every prompt with --ar 4:5 (portrait, roughly phone-shaped mood images).

Converge, then curate

  1. Round 1: run the shot lists as written. Treat the prompts as starting points — chase anything that surprises you.
  2. Round 2 (the pro move): pick your 3–4 favorites in a direction, drag them all into the prompt bar as Style References, and re-run the shot list. Everything now comes out belonging to one world. Real reference photos from Phase 2 work as style references too — often better.
  3. Curate: end with 6–8 keepers per direction, Enhanced/Upscaled, dropped into the right assets/brand-v2 folder. Kill anything that fails: Would NYT ship this? Is the wink present? Does it flatter the real artifact? Does it still work squinted at, at phone size?
Optional power move: Midjourney's Moodboards (under Personalize) let you build a named taste profile per direction by rating images. Worth it only if round 2 isn't converging — otherwise skip.

Phase 4 · Hand over the keepers

Your effort: one message · Claude's effort: overnight

Tell Claude the keepers are in. For each direction Claude will extract, from the actual pixels: a hex palette, material and lighting rules, real (free) typeface pairings, motion words, and a banned list — then rebuild three home screens using your keepers as set dressing and the real artifact plates as content. Judged side-by-side at phone size against a real NYT Games screenshot. No more empty rooms.

Phase 5 · The decision gate

30 minutes · at the pub, on your phone

Pick one world. The losers donate organs (Marble Halls' stately motion, the Curator's wit) but one governs. The test is unchanged: open it on your phone away from your desk and ask, "would I pay for this?"

Phase 6 · Recraft — manufacture the winner

One evening · 1–2 hours

Recraft is where the winning world becomes a consistent, production-ready kit. Unlike Midjourney it outputs true vectors (SVG), renders text correctly, and — the key feature — lets you create a custom Style from uploaded images so every asset matches.

  1. Create a Style: upload 5–8 keepers (plus your best real references) → Recraft builds a reusable style. Every generation from now on uses it.
  2. Generate the kit (each as a set, so pieces match):
    • Masthead / logo exploration — including rendering the existing Question Key concept (assets/logo) in the winning world's style, so that loose end gets resolved or retired.
    • The wax seal, in states: dull brass → pressed deep burgundy.
    • UI ornaments as vectors: hairline frames, dividers, corner marks, lot-number styling.
    • Background textures: paper / marble / leather — subtle, export as PNG.
    • If the Curator's Room wins: a Reggie set — 5 poses (welcome, correct, wrong, streak, trophy) generated against the Style plus his first portrait as an image reference, for consistency.
  3. Export: SVG for anything that sits in the UI (scales perfectly, tiny files), PNG at 2× for textures. Recraft has background removal built in.
Rule: no asset enters the app raw. Everything passes through the brief — recolored to the exact palette, stroke weights matched. Claude enforces this at build time.

Phase 7 · Build

Claude's job · overnight loops · you review at gates

Design tokens from the brief, home screen first, then game screens ported one at a time, then audits. Same gate structure as before — except this time the gates are guarding taste you chose from a hundred options, not taste a model guessed at.

Lock up when you leave.
