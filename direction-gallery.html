The Direction Gallery

Three complete Chronicles, built from your taste interview. Each is a world, not a theme: the same app, imagined by three different design houses.

How to judge: lean back and take each phone in at a glance before reading anything. Then open the NYT Games app on your phone and hold it next to each one — the only question that matters is “would this hold its head up beside the real thing?” Rate each world, note what sings and what's off, and make the call at the bottom. A blend is a legitimate answer.

Direction I

The Curator’s Room

After hours, a side door is left unlocked. Inside: the collection the public never sees — brass labels, green-black shadow, un-exhibited treasures on baize. Someone who clearly loves this place has laid out four games for you on the study table. Candlelight, quiet laughter, the smell of stone and old paper.

Borrowed from: cabinet of curiosities, engraved plates, specimen labels, your curator’s world — the wink at full strength.

Direction II

The House

A great house of history — part Sotheby’s, part Soho House. Ivory pages, one perfect serif, a single line of oxblood. The objects are the drama; the design refuses to compete with them. Today’s games are today’s lots. Viewing closes at midnight.

Borrowed from: Sotheby’s catalogues, Soho House restraint, the Tenement Museum’s focus on the thing itself. Your 5/5 instinct.

Direction III

The Marble Halls

The Louvre at opening time: stone, symmetry, gilt frames, morning light down a long gallery. Each game is a room; each day, the museum rehangs itself. It is grand the way only institutions that have outlived empires can be — and then the little placard asks you, politely, not to use flash.

Borrowed from: the Louvre & British Museum — wall plaques, gallery medallions, engraved capitals, axial symmetry.
