No accounts, no cookies

Yesternerd doesn't have accounts or sign-in. Your scores, streaks and settings live in your browser's local storage, on your own device. Uninstalling the app or clearing site data deletes them for good; nobody else can do that for you, and nobody else can see them. One thing does leave your device — the anonymous score comparison, described in full just below.

The score comparison

When you finish one of today's games, your results screen can tell you how your score compares with everyone else's — "Your 84 beat about 7 in 10 of today's players." To make that line possible, the app sends exactly four things to our server: the issue number, which game, your score, and a meaningless random token. That's the complete list. No name, no email, no account, nothing you typed.

What's kept is an anonymous tally — literally "three people scored 84 on issue 42's Relic". There are no records about individual players: no names, no IP addresses or device details written by us, no per-person timestamps. The token exists only so the same finish isn't counted twice that day; it's newly random every issue and is swept away afterwards, so Monday's player can never be linked to Tuesday's.

One honest caveat: Cloudflare, which hosts the app and the tally, processes request details like your IP address in transit — the way any host on the internet does to deliver a response. We never receive or store them.

The score comparison is currently on for this device.

The same switch lives in the app's home-screen footer ("Compare scores"). Off means nothing is sent at all — not sent and hidden, sent and ignored, or any other half-measure.

What the analytics collect

Yesternerd uses GoatCounter, a small EU-hosted analytics service built for exactly this: no cookies, no cross-site tracking, no advertising network, and no personal identifiers of any kind. It doesn't set a unique ID for your device or try to recognise you between visits.

What it does record is anonymous counts of named events — things like "a game was started" or "a daily was finished" — so the counts can be tallied into a simple funnel (visits → games started → games finished). It never receives your guesses, the names of figures you've typed, or any answer content. A single word summarises the whole policy: counts, not people.

Analytics are currently on for this device.

It sets the same on-device flag as tapping the small version number in the app's home-screen footer five times quickly (that shortcut still works too). This switch only changes whether GoatCounter runs at all — the score comparison above has its own switch.
