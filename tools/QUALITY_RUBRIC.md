# Chronicle content quality rubric

The standard every content batch is held to — at intake and in audits.
Audience bar (see memory: chronicle-target-audience): a Rest-is-History
podcast listener. Easy ≠ dumbed down, hard ≠ academic. Every item should
leave the player feeling a little cleverer, never quizzed at.

The one-line law: **a great item creates an "aha", a good item creates a
nod, a bad item creates a shrug or an argument.** Cut the shrugs, fix the
arguments.

---

## Thread (connections boards)

A board is a *machine for producing one specific feeling*: "oh, THAT's how
they go together." The 16 tiles are the misdirection; the 4 labels are the
punchline.

**Score each board 1–10 on:**

1. **Misdirection (the soul of the game).** Do at least 3–4 tiles
   plausibly belong to a second group on first read? A board where each
   group is an island solves itself four-tiles-at-a-time and plays like a
   sorting chore. Best-in-class: every group shares a surface with another
   (battles that could be sieges, kings that could be title-holders).
2. **The reveal teaches.** After solving, does the player learn one true,
   surprising thing? ("Kandahar is named after Alexander" is worth the
   whole board.) Trivia-recall groups ("Russian rulers") earn their place
   only as the easy anchor, never as the punchline group.
3. **Label wit.** Labels are read at the moment of triumph — they should
   land like a caption, not a taxonomy. "Ideas they gave us" > "Concepts
   in biology". No label should be a Wikipedia category name.
4. **Fairness.** Exactly one defensible home per tile. "Defensible" is
   judged adversarially: if a well-read player can argue a tile into
   another group *using facts that are true*, the board is broken — even
   if the intended solution is "more" correct.
5. **Ladder.** Yellow→purple should climb: yellow = warm-up anyone gets,
   purple = the twist (wordplay, hidden connection, category-of-categories).
   A purple that is just "harder trivia" is a wasted purple.

**Instant flags (fix or cut):**
- Title leaks a tile or a label (a title should set a mood, not answer).
- Elimination-solvable: three groups so easy the fourth needs no thought.
- Ambiguous tile with two true homes (the #1 trust-killer — players
  remember being "wrongly wronged" far longer than they remember a win).
- Factual wobble in a label's claim about its tiles (e.g. a military
  engineer filed under "ministers").
- Two groups on the same axis ("X named Henry" + "X named Louis" is one
  idea wearing two colours).
- Group requires niche-academic recall (regnal numbers of minor dynasties,
  historiographical jargon) — that's "hard = academic", which we reject.

## Face Value (portraits) & Relic (artefacts)

The game is a striptease of recognition: the crop opens on ambiguity and
resolves into fame. Quality = the *gasp gap* — the distance between "no
idea" at 15% and "OF COURSE" at 100%.

**Score each item 1–10 on:**

1. **Fame floor.** At full reveal, would the target player recognise it or
   at least go "ah, I've *seen* this"? An item nobody recognises even
   revealed scores zero fun at every difficulty. (Fame of the *image*,
   not the person: Rutherford is famous, his face is not — that's what
   difficulty tiers are for, not a reason to cut.)
2. **Crop ambiguity.** Does the opening fragment genuinely mislead or at
   least withhold? (See memory: image-game-crop-calibration — difficulty =
   fame × fragment ambiguity; never open on the money shot.)
3. **Answer fairness (variants).** EVERY name a reasonable player would
   type must be in `variants`: nicknames, anglicisations, "the X of Y"
   forms, common misspellings. A right answer marked wrong is the single
   worst moment the app can produce.
4. **Blurb payoff.** The blurb is the reward text. One concrete, delightful
   fact beats dates-and-titles. Dates must be correct; museum/location
   must be current.
5. **Difficulty honesty.** easy = instantly famous image; medium = famous
   subject, less-worn image; hard = earned recognition. Miscalibration
   poisons the daily recipe (RECIPE hands easy slots to Monday players).

**Instant flags:** caption/steganography leaking the answer inside the
visible crop; wrong-thing images (interior when the fame is the exterior);
living politicians (banned outright); blurb factual errors.

## Lifeline (map figures)

1. **Name recognition** to the audience bar — the game is dead if the
   player's reaction is "who?". Bias toward monarchs/statesmen/commanders
   (see mapgame-audit-plan).
2. **Journey interest.** The birth→death arc is the content. Great:
   Napoleon (Corsica → Saint Helena). Flat: born and died in the same
   city — acceptable only for very famous figures.
3. **Data truth.** Years, places, coords. A wrong pin is a fact error a
   map-lover WILL notice.
4. **Variants completeness** — same law as Face Value.

## Force-ranking protocol (how audits use this)

Rate → rank within difficulty tier → bottom 15% gets adjudicated by a
senior pass: *fix* if one repair restores it, *cut* if the concept is the
problem. Never grade on a curve upward: a tier where everything is genuinely
good cuts nothing.

Array order in the data files IS the airing order (daily.js cursors walk
each tier's array). Therefore: **array order = quality order, best first.**
The first weeks a player experiences must be the certified best we have.
