[NEW RELIC RULES — apply to everything, now and forever]
1. Specific thing, not a type of thing. (A named historic individual aircraft
   could be in; the model line is out.)
2. Modern buildings out: anything built in the last 10-20 years, and anything
   legitimately called contemporary. (His vetoes extended this to modern
   skyscrapers generally, incl. 1970s towers.)
3. Whole cities/towns/villages/countries OUT; buildings, sculptures, ruins of a
   specific building, parts of a building IN. For famous ancient sites: the more
   famous, the more a SPECIFIC PART should stand in for the whole (Villa of the
   Mysteries in, Pompeii out). Less-famous non-Western sites may stay wholesale
   (Gedi). Tie-break: iconic single image/viewpoint or extreme distinctiveness
   from any angle → in (Cahokia Mounds in; answer accepts "cahokia").
   The test: "if a user sees an image of this, will they reasonably think this
   is a fair inclusion in Relic?"

[AMENDMENT 24 Jul] Chichen Itza stays IN WHOLESALE (no swap to El Castillo) —
passes the tie-break: iconic image represents the whole site.

[ARTICULATION] The tie-break, stated cleanly ("the postcard test"): does the
name evoke exactly ONE picture in most minds? If the site and a single image
are fused (Chichen Itza = El Castillo view, Machu Picchu = terrace view),
wholesale inclusion is fair. If the name evokes an area/experience with no
single image (Pompeii, Cappadocia), use the iconic specific part instead.

[AMENDMENT 2] Teotihuacan ALSO stays IN WHOLESALE (no swap to Pyramid of the
Sun). Refined principle: it's about what the name MEANS in common usage —
"Teotihuacan" and "Chichen Itza" effectively denote 1-2 specific buildings,
so the term and the image line up. "Pompeii" denotes a sprawling area — too
big, no alignment. Swap-to-part only when the name denotes more than the
picture can show.

[CANONICAL TEST — supersedes all prior articulations] Player's perspective,
two parts. Shown the famous image: (1) FAIRNESS — will the player think
"yes, that's a fair Relic puzzle"? (2) ANSWER EXPECTATION — will they
reasonably expect the site/common name to be accepted, or would they expect
to need the specific building/ruin/artefact name? Inclusion AND answer
aliases both follow from what the player would expect, not from taxonomy.
Consequence: where site and famous part are fused, accept BOTH as answers
(Chichen Itza / El Castillo).

[AMENDMENT 3] Petra ALSO stays IN WHOLESALE — by the canonical test, the
famous image (Treasury facade) makes players expect "Petra" as the answer.
Aliases accept "al-khazneh"/"treasury" too. Pompeii remains the lone swap
(→ Villa of the Mysteries, alias "pompeii").

[ARTICULATION 2 — "count the postcards"] Petra has ONE iconic image (Treasury
from the Siq) → name and image fused → wholesale in. Pompeii has MANY iconic
images (wall paintings, casts, streets), each of something IN Pompeii, not OF
Pompeii → name bigger than any picture → must name the specific thing shown.
Consistent with the canonical player test; useful as the mechanical tiebreak.
