Chronicle — a new mark

Six concepts. One idea per mark, ink-first, gold as garnish. Each is shown at 512 / 88 / 40 / 16 px, in the app's real masthead, on ink, and in pure monochrome. Then the cull, a refinement round for the survivors, and a third round pushing the winning key from “utility” toward “play”.

Killed: perfect radial symmetry · emblems-in-rings · ornament without an idea.

Part two · The cull

Judged as a critic: second read · 40 px · smile · distinctiveness. “Nice” is a failing grade.

01 · Unfinished C Arrives, but faintly — it reads “swoosh” before it reads “pen”. The taper mushes; the ink drop becomes dust at 16. A polite nod, not a smile. No. The crowded-C graveyard: fintech, yogurt, consultancies. Killed
02 · Sundial Page Genuine two-beat: a note page… oh, it’s a sundial. Fold + shadow hold; the ticks die first — fixable. Yes — quiet wit that rewards the second look. Yes. Nobody owns “paper that tells time”. Advances
03 · Column Pencil A triple: newspaper column, classical column, pencil. Lands fast, but lands. Best in class — verticals survive even 16. Yes; the pun keeps paying on repeat viewings. Mostly — must dodge “education clip-art” with proportion and tilt. Advances
04 · Coin-Slot Sunrise The coin-slot read never arrives uninvited; strangers see a sunrise and stop. Fine — but it’s a horizontal strip trapped in a square favicon. Mild. No. A rising sun over a rule is a regional bank at dawn. Killed
05 · Confetti Hourglass Yes, but inherited — it’s the old logo in a party hat. Confetti becomes dandruff at 40; a smudge at 16. Party-store cheer, not wit. Hourglass = the great time-brand cliché. Museum gift shop. Killed
06 · C Key True hidden-letter wit: a key first, a C a beat later. The silhouette is bulletproof down to 16. Yes — it is literally a charm. Keys skew locksmith/heraldry; the tilt and round terminals pull it back to play. Advances

Why 01 dies

The concept sentence is good; the silhouette isn’t. Strip the story away and you’re left with a tapering open circle — the most contested shape in trademark history. If a mark needs its caption to be distinct, it isn’t distinct. The nib flick is the best part and it is 8% of the drawing.

Why 04 dies

The wit lives entirely in a two-pixel gap in the top rule. Wit that needs a footnote isn’t wit. First read is “sunrise over a divider”, which is halfway to a savings-and-loan. It also composes as a landscape strip, so the favicon wastes half its box. The one thing it does well — belonging to the masthead — the keyline variation (06·R2) does better.

Why 05 dies

Honest test, honest failure. It inherits the astrolabe’s problem in a new costume: an emblem about time rather than a charm about play. And confetti is a texture, not a gesture — at card-glyph size it reads as noise, and noise is the opposite of this design system.

The three survivors, each pushed through two variations.

Part four · Round three — making the key play

Client note on the charm: “reads as a utility — lodgings, locksmiths — not a game. I should feel drawn in to play.” Fair, and precisely put: the charm is an object, and objects read as services. Games are moments and invitations. Five ways to put play into the key.

Part five · Verdict & files

Primary — 06·Q1 · The Question Key. Round three’s answer to the client note: a question mark cut like a key. Quiz first, key second — the mark now promises a daily question and the unlock, not a locksmith. It gave up the hidden C after the mirrored version failed on screen (the designer’s note tells that story honestly); the C belongs to the wordmark, and the mark belongs to the game. A question mark is the most legible favicon a quiz app can own, and the keyline (06·R2) still works beside it as the masthead rule.

Held in reserve: the quiet charm (06·R1) for contexts where the mark must whisper; 03 · The Column Pencil as the strongest non-key mark; the Sundial-Page solid token if the home-screen icon ever wants a filled shape. The round-two verdict below the cull stands as the record of how we got here.
